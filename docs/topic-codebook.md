# Topic Codebook

Interpretive labels assigned to the six latent topics (T1–T6) identified via
LDA (k=6), with the top terms used to justify each label.

## T1 — Walkability & Built Environment

**Top terms:** walkability, street, design, walkable, pedestrian

**Interpretation:** Captures literature on pedestrian infrastructure, street
design, and the physical/built-environment conditions that support or
hinder walking.

## T2 — Compact City & Sustainable Form

**Top terms:** compact, land, spatial, sustainable, sprawl

**Interpretation:** Represents the compact city tradition — urban form,
land-use intensity, and sustainability framed through density and
anti-sprawl planning.

## T3 — Transit-Oriented Development

**Top terms:** transit, station, TOD, railway, intensity

**Interpretation:** Covers transit-oriented development literature centred
on rail/station-area planning and density around transit infrastructure.

## T4 — Housing & Land Markets

**Top terms:** housing, rural, dwelling, redevelopment, stock

**Interpretation:** Addresses housing supply, land markets, and
redevelopment dynamics, including rural-urban housing stock considerations.

## T5 — X-Min City & Proximity Planning

**Top terms:** minute, accessibility, concept, transport, service

**Interpretation:** The core X-minute/15-minute city literature — proximity
to daily services, accessibility as an organizing concept, and neighbourhood-
scale transport-service relationships. This topic overtakes T2 (Compact City)
as the dominant frame from 2021 onward (see Findings, main manuscript).

## T6 — Health, Wellbeing & Urban Environment

**Top terms:** health, physical activity, exposure, air quality, pollution

**Interpretation:** Connects proximity-based planning to public health
outcomes — physical activity, environmental exposure, and air quality as
consequences or correlates of urban form.

## Labelling method

Topic labels were assigned by the authors through qualitative interpretation
of each topic's top-weighted terms (ranked by probability within the LDA
model), cross-referenced against the broader proximity-based urban planning
literature to ensure labels reflect established terminology in the field.
